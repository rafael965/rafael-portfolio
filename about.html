How I got here

I started in customer operations at Amazon, moved into administrative management at a digital forensics firm, then into construction project coordination. Different industries, the same recurring discovery: the thing slowing the business down was almost never a lack of talent. It was that nobody had written the process down.

So I started writing them down. Then I started noticing which documented steps a computer should be doing instead — and, eventually, building those.

The training was already there. I hold a Computer Programming NC IV and was named Programmer of the Year in 2017, which felt like an irrelevant detail through most of the administrative years. It stopped being irrelevant the moment a spreadsheet stopped coping and the honest answer was “this should be an application.”

The most recent chapter is legal operations — client coordination and executive support at an intellectual property firm, where the same pattern applies: intake, onboarding, matter administration and compliance, held together by documentation and improved with automation where automation genuinely earns its place.

The part I care about is handover. A system that only works while I'm running it isn't a system — it's a dependency. Everything on this site was built to be given away.
